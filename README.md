# Adekola Owoyemi

PhD Candidate, Protein Evolution Lab (Casola Lab), Ecology & Conservation Biology Program, Texas A&M University. Defending in 2026 and open to postdoc opportunities in comparative and computational genomics.

I study how new genes and proteins emerge and evolve, using comparative genomics, computational methods, and machine learning. My dissertation focuses on the identification and evolutionary analysis of de novo genes.

## Research

- De novo gene identification and evolution
- Protein evolution, including noncanonical proteins and minor annotated isoforms
- Comparative and computational genomics across multiple species
- Machine learning methods for gene and isoform prediction

## Featured projects

Two stages of **LINGUA**, my dissertation's comparative genomics framework for de novo gene identification:

- **[Gene Stratigraphy Pipeline](https://github.com/Ludtson/gene-stratigraphy-pipeline)** — classifies genes by phylogenetic depth of origin from OrthoFinder's orthology output, producing candidate lineage-specific genes with a mandatory cross-check against structural blind spots in HOG-based classification.
- **[Protein Preprocessing & Isoform Selection Pipeline](https://github.com/Ludtson/protein-preprocessing-isoform-pipeline)** — protein-level quality control and longest-isoform selection across multi-species GFF/protein FASTA datasets. Used in my dissertation work on Brassicaceae de novo gene analysis.
